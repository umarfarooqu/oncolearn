Understanding the 10 Features

These measurements analyze cell nuclei from a fine needle aspirate to identify cancer patterns

Radius Mean (μm)

The average distance from the center of a cell nucleus to its outer perimeter. Malignant cells often have larger, swollen nuclei compared to healthy cells.

Perimeter Mean (μm)

The average total distance around the outside boundary of the cell nuclei. This is another way to gauge the overall size and expansion of the cells.

Area Mean (μm²)

The average total surface area of the cell nuclei. A significantly larger area is a strong primary indicator of abnormal cell growth.

Smoothness Mean

Measures the local variation in the radius lengths. It evaluates if the edge of the nucleus is smooth and even, or jagged and varying in length.

Compactness Mean

Compares the perimeter and area to determine how closely the nucleus resembles a perfect circle. Malignant cells are often irregularly shaped and less compact.

Concavity Mean

Measures the severity or depth of inward curves (indentations or "valleys") on the boundary of the nucleus. Deep indentations indicate malignancy.

Concave Points Mean

Counts the average number of inward-curving points or indentations along the cell border. More points indicate more irregularity.

Symmetry Mean

Evaluates how symmetrical the cell nucleus is. Healthy cells are typically highly symmetrical, while cancerous cells are often lopsided.

Texture Mean

The standard deviation of grayscale values within the nucleus image. It measures variation in light and dark pixels. Rough, varied texture is a sign of abnormality.

Fractal Dimension Mean

Measures the complexity and chaos of the nucleus boundary (like a coastline approximation). Higher values indicate rougher, more jagged edges common in cancer cells.

How Your RandomForestClassifier Works

Your RandomForestClassifier looks at all 10 of these measurements simultaneously. While a single irregular feature (like a slightly larger radius) might not indicate cancer, the model builds a "forest" of decision trees to see how these features interact.

If a cell has a large area, deep concavities, high texture variation, and irregular symmetry, the model recognizes that specific combination as a high-risk pattern. By analyzing these 10 interconnected features, the model can distinguish between benign and malignant cells with remarkable accuracy (97% baseline).
